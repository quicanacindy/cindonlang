## Writing System (한글)                                             2026.11.03
The first thing you need to learn when studying any language is its writing system. Korean is based on Hangul (한글), the Korean alphabet.
Hangul letters (자모) are combined to form syllable blocks (음절). Each block consists of 2 to 4 letters (자모).
예) 개 (2) ; 빵 (3) ; 닭 (3)

## Numbers (숫자)
There are two numbering systems in Korean:
1). Sino-Korean Numbers (한자어 수)
Based on Chinese numerals. Used for dates, money, minutes, phone numbers, addresses, heights, lengths, and generally for numbers from 100 onwards.
(picture)
2). Native Korean Numbers (고유어 수)
Based on traditional Korean numbers. Used for general counting, such as hours, people, objects, and age. Generally used for numbers from 1 to 99.
(picture)
However, when it comes to counting, knowing only the Native Korean number system is not enough. Korean also uses counter words (단위 명사 / 세는 단위), which are used to count different types of objects, people, animals, etc.
예) Counting objects, people, and animals requires different counter words.

## Word Order and Sentence Structure (어순과 문장 구조)
English: S-V-O
Korean: S-O-V
In Korean sentences, the verb comes at the end and plays a pivotal role in determining the meaning of everything stated before it.

**-(으)ㄹ 때** = when ...: 커피를 마실 때 (when I drink coffee).

## Details of korean

In korean, the establishment that sells coffee, café, is said 카페 (kkape), meanwhile the product itself is said 커피 (kkopi). 

Connectors commonly used in articles: 
그래서… ⇾ that’s why… / so…

Places with 에 and 에서
- **에** = destination or where something exists: 카페에 가요 (I go to the café).
- **에서** = where an action happens: 카페에서 이야기해요 (I talk at the café).
